METHOD­OLOGY

01 What the page does

Doppelgängers don't exist. But what about anagranames: people whose name has the same letters as yours?

You type a name. The page lists the full names (one first name and one last name) that contain exactly the letters of your name. It shows first the full names that real people have, most people first (see 07), then the other full names, most probable first. It gives the countries where people with your name live (see 08).

Each first name and each last name comes from population data. Most pairs of a first name and a last name are new, and a model gives the probability of each pair.

02 Data

name_dataset [1]: 491,655,925 records (first name, last name, gender, country) from 106 countries, extracted from the Facebook leak of 2021 (533 million accounts). We keep the names in Latin script that occur at least 5 times in the world, and the people whose two names are kept. This is the only count filter, against fake names: the model and all the files keep these names in each country where they occur, and all the full names of these people.

03 Letters

The key of a name is its letters A to Z: the accents are removed, the case is ignored, and the spaces, hyphens and apostrophes are removed. Two names are anagrams when their keys contain the same letters. The signature of a name is its key with the letters in alphabetical order.

04 Search

The names are stored in a trie of their signatures: a tree where each node adds one letter, and where the names with the same signature are on the same node. Signatures share their first letters, so the trie has about one node per name.

The search goes down the trie of first names and enters a node only if the query has a free copy of its letter. Thus it visits only the first names made of letters of the query. For each first name that it finds, the remaining letters, in alphabetical order, give one path down the trie of last names. The node at the end of this path holds all the last names that complete the query. A search takes a few milliseconds.

05 Ranking

For a first name $f$ and a last name $l$ in a country $c$, the base is the probability of two independent names, from the counts $N_{fc}$ of people with the name $f$ in $c$:

$$p_0(f,l\mid c)=p(f\mid c)\,p(l\mid c),\qquad p(f\mid c)=\frac{N_{fc}+\alpha\,\pi_f}{N_c+\alpha}$$

with $N_c$ the people of $c$, $\pi_f$ the share of $f$ in the world and $\alpha=10^5$. The model adds a term for the pair:

$$\log p(f,l\mid c)=\log p_0(f,l\mid c)+\mathbb{1}[(f,l)\in S_c]\,\bigl(u_f^\top \operatorname{diag}(g_c)\,v_l+b_c\bigr)-\log Z_c$$

Each name has a vector of 16 numbers ($u_f$, $v_l$), learned from 282 million people. The product $u_f^\top \operatorname{diag}(g_c)\,v_l$ is large when the two names go together, for example a Portuguese first name with a Portuguese last name. The weights $g_c$ and the offset $b_c$ are specific to the country. $S_c$ contains the pairs of names that both occur in $c$, and $Z_c$ makes the probabilities of all pairs sum to 1.

In the world, the probability is the mixture of the countries, with $w_c=P_c/P$ the share of $c$ in the population $P$ of the 105 countries (World Bank, 2023):

$$p(f,l)=\sum_c w_c\,p(f,l\mid c)$$

The weights do not come from name_dataset: it has 0.09% of its people in Japan and 4.9% in France, but Japan has 1.9% of the population of the 105 countries and France 1.0%.

On people that the model did not see in training, the model needs 0.467 bits per person less than $p_0$, and it is better than $p_0$ in each of the 105 countries. The paper gives the details.

06 The number “1 in N”

$N=1/p(f,l\mid c)$. It is the expected share of people in the country with this exact full name. A full name can be “1 in 3 billion” in a country of 17 million people: the model expects no person with this full name, but the two names exist.

In the world, $N=1/p(f,l)$ is a share of the 6.6 billion people of the 105 countries. The world expects at least as many people with a full name as each of its countries: $P\,p(f,l)\ge P_c\,p(f,l\mid c)$. For example, Yuki Tanaka is 1 in 13,182 in Japan (9,446 people expected) and 1 in 696,584 in the world (9,474 people expected).

07 Real names

A result is marked real when people of name_dataset have this exact full name in the selected country, with their number. The real full names come first in the list, the full names with the most people first. The headline adds the people of the marked names. name_dataset contains only a part of the population, and it does not remove duplicates: one person with several accounts counts several times.

In the world, the page marks the 154.8 million full names of name_dataset, with their people in all countries together. Thus a full name with 11 accounts in 7 countries, one of them in France, is real with 11 people in the world and real with 1 person in France. These full names, with their people in each country, are in 4,096 files of about 113 KB (465 MB together). The letters of the query give its file (the sum of a number per letter, modulo 4,096), and this file contains all the full names made of these letters.

Click a result for links to the pages of its first name, its last name and the full name (in a new tab): their people in the world and in each country, or in the selected country.

08 Where people with your name live

In the world, the page splits your query into a first name and a last name of the data and gives the probability of each country for this full name:

$$p(c\mid f,l)=\frac{w_c\,p(f,l\mid c)}{\sum_{c'} w_{c'}\,p(f,l\mid c')}$$

It is an estimate of the model from the frequency of each name in each country, not the count of the accounts with this full name: a full name can get a large share in a country where its last name is frequent, without any account with this full name there. It is the country of the accounts, not the origin of the name. Both names must be in the world file (see 10).

09 France from INSEE

The INSEE counts give $p(f)$ and $p(l)$ directly. The term for the pair comes from a model trained on the French people of name_dataset only, which is better for France than the world model (0.763 against 0.42 bits per person, each against its own base):

$$\log p(f,l)=\log\frac{N_f}{N}+\log\frac{N_l}{N'}+u_f^\top v_l-\log Z$$

The real names of France from INSEE are the full names of France in name_dataset (see 07) whose two names are in the INSEE files.

10 Country and files

At the first visit, the page selects the country of your first browser language that has a country (en-GB: United Kingdom; fr: France from INSEE), else the world. You can search a country by its name in English, in your language or in its own language, with typing errors.

All the sources but France from INSEE read the same files: the world keeps all the names of the data (1,724,039 first names and 3,456,059 last names), with their people in each country. A country is the world restricted to the names seen in it, with the people of the country: its results are the same as those of a file of the country, and the world file is downloaded once for all the countries. Each vector is compressed to 8 bytes by product quantization, which changes the result by 0.006 bits per person.

The world is too large for one file (73 MB). The world file contains the trie and the people of each name. The display forms, the people per country and the vectors of the names are in 5,060 files of 1,024 names (about 14 KB each), in the order of the trie. The trie sorts the letters of each name from the rarest letter (Q, X, Z...) to the most frequent one, thus a search skips all the names with a letter that is not in the query, and the names that it finds are in few files. After the trie, a search downloads the files of the names that it finds (85 files, 1.5 MB, for a median query) and the file of its real full names (see 07). Files already downloaded are not downloaded again. France from INSEE is one file with all its data.

11 Privacy

The search and the ranking run in your browser: the name you type is not sent to a server. Except for France from INSEE, the page downloads the files of the names made of your letters (see 10) and the file of real full names of your letters (see 07): the server can see which files they are, thus which letters your name can contain. The files contain, for each first name and each last name, the number of people per country and the vector, and the full names of the people of name_dataset with their number per country, and no other data about them (no gender, no account, no contact data).

12 Limits

name_dataset comes from profiles of a social network: it contains typing errors, nicknames, words that are not names, and names from other countries. Only Latin script is kept. INSEE gives only the names of people born in France, for the periods above. The probabilities are estimates of a model.
